01 / A little daydream

Not just a road trip.
A whole chapter.

Route 66 is the legendary road that once connected Chicago to Santa Monica. It crosses the heart of America through small towns, neon-lit main streets, wide-open desert and places that feel like they have been waiting for you to arrive. The original highway was decommissioned, so today the adventure is about following its historic alignments, choosing your own detours and letting the road surprise you.

05 / Who's in the passenger seat?

One road.
Two possibilities.

This trip doesn't need a confirmed plus-one to become a plan. Build the dream for yourself first. If your friend comes along, brilliant — there'll be a second set of eyes for the sunsets and someone to argue with over the playlist. If not, the road is still yours.

A note for the itinerary

Plan around your own pace, comfort and budget. Choose well-reviewed places to stay, share your route with someone at home, and give yourself permission to change the plan.

THE TRAVEL RULE

Make room for yourself.

A solo road trip can be full of people — the diner owner who tells you a story, the stranger who recommends a hidden viewpoint, the friendly face at a motel. Keep safety practical: drive in daylight where possible, book accommodation ahead, and don't share your live location publicly.

06 / Before we go

A few things to know.

Give it time

About 2–3 weeks is a more relaxed starting point for a long westbound drive with meaningful stops. More time means less rushing.

Check the route

Route 66 is no longer one continuous signed highway. Use a dedicated historic-route map and check closures, road conditions and detours.

Sort the paperwork

As a South African traveller, check current US visa requirements, passport validity, driving rules and rental-car age/insurance conditions before booking.

Budget for the real trip

Allow for flights, car hire, fuel, accommodation, food, insurance, attractions and an emergency buffer. Prices vary by season and travel style.
